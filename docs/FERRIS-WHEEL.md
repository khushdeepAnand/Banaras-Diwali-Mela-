# Ferris wheel · Phase 3B / 3B.1–3B.2 camera polish

The first amusement attraction is entirely procedural. `frontend/src/world/FerrisWheel.tsx` builds paired A-frames, an axle, two deep rim assemblies, instanced spokes, emissive bulbs and twelve open-sided cabins with floors, seats, rails and roofs. A small booth with an original canvas sign and a railed platform mark the boarding point. No external assets, downloads, physics engine, or extra dynamic lights are required. Low graphics retains the complete ride and omits only small cabin trim; high mode uses the existing scene shadow settings.

## Placement and navigation

The wheel is centered at **x=15.5, z=5**, radius **5.1** world units, axle y=7.25, on the lower right ghat terrace. The boarding marker is **(15.5, 8.35)**; the safe exit is **(15.5, 8.6)**. The original spawn (0, 10.5), riverfront and six stalls at z≈16.65 remain in place. Only the decorative pillar formerly at x=16, z=13.5 was moved to x=20, z=13.5, and a lamp formerly at x=18, z=7.5 was moved to x=20, z=10.5 so they do not obstruct the new boarding route. Walk from the spawn toward the right edge of the ghat, then toward the river to reach the queue.

`environment.ts` excludes a simple rectangular sweep/support footprint, the booth, and the platform's side rails while leaving the center boarding approach open. Walking uses the same substep/axis-slide collision as the rest of the ghat; the wheel is not a physics simulation. Decorated ground, some roof edges and small cabin pieces are not individually collidable. Follow-camera obstruction is approximate outside the existing stall protections.

## Ride and camera

`ride.ts` owns typed coordinates, the interaction radius, wheel speed (0.26 radians/second) and pure angle/seat-selection helpers. React tracks only phase changes: **approaching → boarding → riding → exiting → approaching**. A frame-loop ref drives the wheel, chooses the cabin closest to the bottom at boarding, pauses rotation for a 2.1-second boarding transition, and counter-rotates each cabin against the wheel so it stays upright. Exiting accelerates toward the next bottom stop, pauses briefly to return the visitor to the platform, and restores walking. The ride can be repeated; Reset returns directly to spawn. Rotation and camera movement never update React state every frame; a slow height readout is the only ride HUD telemetry.

The player model moves with the selected cabin and normal movement is locked while on the ride. The walking `Player` camera yields to the dedicated `RideCamera.ts` controller; on exit the walking camera resumes smoothly using the preserved yaw/pitch. The gondola pivot is at z=3.55, the spokes and rims occupy z≈4.2–5.8, and the original camera at z≈0.45 aimed toward z=10: its line of sight went directly through the axle and spokes. **The corrected camera stays behind the rear rim at z=7.8**, around 1.15–1.25 units above the selected gondola, while its x coordinate follows the selected gondola. The low view looks toward the stalls, lamps, and ghat route. As the cabin rises, the viewpoint and gaze ease toward a more riverward, elevated panorama. A cached raycaster checks this optional sightline against the wheel's mechanical group every 0.25 seconds (not every frame); if a passing spoke or cabin obstructs it, the controller smoothly returns to the guaranteed clear market-side view. Only wheel meshes are raycast, not every decoration in the world. Camera vectors and the raycaster are reused.

Phase 3B.2 makes framing aspect-aware without changing the ride path: the elevated portrait look target eases downward by up to 3.3 units as the cabin rises, and portrait ride FOV blends to 76° rather than 79°. This reduces empty sky while retaining the horizon. Landscape camera and look-target x coordinates ease westward with cabin elevation, pushing the near-side rim farther toward the frame edge. These offsets remain relative to the **moving selected gondola** and are smoothed through the existing frame-loop follow; boarding/exiting waypoints, state transitions, wheel speed and geometry are unchanged.

Boarding follows three smooth waypoints, all on the **z≥7.8** side of the rear rim, from the walking camera via the west side to the selected gondola's outboard view. Disembarking reverses that clear-side path and returns to the platform. Neither path passes through the wheel's z≈4.2–5.8 support volume. The viewpoint is a stabilized virtual offset associated with the moving cabin, not a physical head camera inside the roof, nor an unrelated fixed scenic camera. It intentionally shows little cabin structure so the view remains usable in portrait. Drag-to-look is reserved for walking and resumes on exit. The state-independent sound toggle remains optional; no ride-specific audio or autoplay was added.

## Controls and verification

- Desktop: approach the boarding marker; press **E** or select **Board the Ferris wheel**. Press **E** or select **Exit ride** while riding. W/A/S/D, arrows, drag-to-look, Reset, stall E interactions and graphics settings remain available on foot.
- Touch: use the existing joystick to reach the marker and tap the boarding prompt; the joystick is replaced by a large Exit ride button while aboard. Toolbar and touch controls respect the existing safe-area CSS.
- Browser tests cover one full rotation, approach, repeated boarding/exiting, the selected cabin and camera coordinates, cabin elevation changes, frozen ground movement, restored drag-to-look, low/high mobile graphics, touch boarding/exiting at 390 × 844, and the pre-existing stalls/riverfront. **Known limitations:** the wheel operates as a demonstration ride without capacity, tickets, queues of NPCs or real-time safety simulation. The outboard viewpoint is a cinematic offset rather than the visitor's literal eyes inside the cabin; it can briefly show a passing decorative lamp or distant cabin near the edge of the view. Raycasts cover the optional panorama against the wheel, not collision with every world decoration. On very slow hardware camera motion and rotation are clamped after long frame stalls. Test performance and legibility on real devices before production use.
